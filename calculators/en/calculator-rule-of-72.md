## Rule of 72 Calculator – Calculate Years to Double Your Money

### What is the Rule of 72?
The **Rule of 72** is a famous financial mental math formula used to estimate the exact number of years required to double an investment at a fixed annual compound interest rate. 

By simply dividing the number **72** by your expected annual rate of return, investors can instantly determine how long it will take for their capital to grow by 100% without needing complex logarithmic calculations.

---

### Formula to Calculate Rule of 72

The mathematical formula for the Rule of 72 is:

$$
\text{Years to Double} \approx \frac{72}{\text{Annual Rate of Return } (r)}
$$

Where:
- **r** = Annual interest or growth rate as a whole percentage (e.g., for $8\%$, $r = 8$)

Alternatively, to calculate the **rate of return required** to double your money in $T$ years:

$$
\text{Required Interest Rate } (r) \approx \frac{72}{\text{Target Years } (T)}
$$

### Step-by-Step Worked Examples

#### Example 1: Calculating Time to Double
Suppose you invest **₹50,000** in a mutual fund with an expected annual return of **9%**:

$$\text{Years to Double} = \frac{72}{9} = 8 \text{ Years}$$

Your ₹50,000 investment will grow to approximately **₹100,000 in 8 years**.

#### Example 2: Calculating Inflation Impact (Purchasing Power Halving)
The Rule of 72 also calculates how fast inflation halves your purchasing power:
If inflation averages **6% per year**:

$$\text{Years to Halve Purchasing Power} = \frac{72}{6} = 12 \text{ Years}$$

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

#### 1. How accurate is the Rule of 72?
The Rule of 72 is remarkably accurate for interest rates between **6% and 10%**. For interest rates outside this window, minor adjustments (such as the Rule of 69 or Rule of 70) provide slightly closer logarithmic approximations.

#### 2. Can the Rule of 72 be used to calculate inflation impact?
Yes. Dividing 72 by the annual inflation rate tells you how many years it will take for your money's purchasing power to cut in half.

#### 3. What is the difference between Rule of 72, Rule of 70, and Rule of 69?
- **Rule of 72**: Best for standard compound rates ($6\% - 10\%$) and easy mental division.
- **Rule of 70**: Best for calculating inflation and population doubling.
- **Rule of 69**: Best for continuously compounded interest.
