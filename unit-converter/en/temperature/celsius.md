## Celsius (°C) Measurement Guide & Temperature Converter

### Definition
**Celsius** (symbol: **°C**), historically known as **centigrade**, is the primary metric scale of temperature used globally across science, medicine, weather forecasting, cooking, and daily life. 

The Celsius scale defines **$0^\circ\text{C}$** as the exact freezing point of water and **$100^\circ\text{C}$** as the exact boiling point of water under standard atmospheric pressure ($1 \text{ atm}$ or $101.325 \text{ kPa}$).

### How to Calculate Celsius Conversions (Formulas & Examples)

To convert between Celsius, Fahrenheit, and Kelvin:

- **Celsius to Fahrenheit:** $^\circ\text{F} = (^\circ\text{C} \times \frac{9}{5}) + 32 = (^\circ\text{C} \times 1.8) + 32$
- **Celsius to Kelvin:** $\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$
- **Fahrenheit to Celsius:** $^\circ\text{C} = (^\circ\text{F} - 32) \times \frac{5}{9} = \frac{^\circ\text{F} - 32}{1.8}$
- **Kelvin to Celsius:** $^\circ\text{C} = \text{K} - 273.15$

#### Calculation Example:
If an oven recipe specifies **$180^\circ\text{C}$**, convert the baking temperature to Fahrenheit and Kelvin:

1. **Fahrenheit Conversion:**  
   $$^\circ\text{F} = (180 \times 1.8) + 32 = 324 + 32 = 356^\circ\text{F}$$

2. **Kelvin Conversion:**  
   $$\text{K} = 180 + 273.15 = 453.15\text{ K}$$

### History and Anders Celsius Origin
The scale was invented in 1742 by Swedish astronomer **Anders Celsius**. Originally, Celsius designed a reverse scale where $0^\circ$ represented the boiling point of water and $100^\circ$ represented the freezing point. Following his death in 1744, botanist Carl Linnaeus inverted the scale to the modern configuration we use today ($0^\circ\text{C}$ freezing, $100^\circ\text{C}$ boiling).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. What is the formula to convert Celsius to Fahrenheit?
Multiply the Celsius temperature by 1.8 and add 32: $^\circ\text{F} = (^\circ\text{C} \times 1.8) + 32$.

#### 2. How do I convert Celsius to Kelvin?
Add 273.15 to the Celsius temperature: $\text{K} = ^\circ\text{C} + 273.15$.

#### 3. At what temperature are Celsius and Fahrenheit equal?
Celsius and Fahrenheit are equal at **$-40^\circ$** (i.e., $-40^\circ\text{C} = -40^\circ\text{F}$).

#### 4. What is absolute zero in Celsius?
Absolute zero (the lowest theoretical temperature where molecular motion ceases) is **$-273.15^\circ\text{C}$** ($0\text{ K}$).

#### 5. What is normal human body temperature in Celsius?
Normal human body temperature is approximately **$37^\circ\text{C}$** ($98.6^\circ\text{F}$).

#### 6. What is the freezing and boiling point of water in Celsius?
Under standard atmospheric pressure, water freezes at **$0^\circ\text{C}$** and boils at **$100^\circ\text{C}$**.

#### 7. Why was centigrade renamed to Celsius?
In 1948, the General Conference on Weights and Measures officially renamed "centigrade" to "Celsius" to eliminate language confusion with the French term for angular measurement (one hundredth of a grade).

#### 8. How do I convert Celsius to Fahrenheit mentally?
Double the Celsius temperature and add 30 for a quick rough estimate. For example, $20^\circ\text{C} \times 2 = 40 + 30 \approx 70^\circ\text{F}$ (exact is $68^\circ\text{F}$).
