## Compound Interest Calculator – Calculate Future Value & Investment Growth

### What is Compound Interest?
**Compound interest** is the interest earned on both the initial principal amount and the accumulated interest from prior compounding periods. Often called *"interest on interest"*, compounding allows investments, savings accounts, and fixed deposits to grow exponentially over time.

Using an online **Compound Interest Calculator** helps investors project long-term wealth growth, compare compounding frequencies (monthly, quarterly, annually), and plan for major financial goals like retirement.

---

### Formula to Calculate Compound Interest

The standard mathematical formula used to compute future value ($A$) with compound interest is:

$$
A = P \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^{n \cdot t}
$$

Where:
- **A** = Total accumulated amount (Principal + Interest)
- **P** = Initial principal investment
- **r** = Annual interest rate in decimal form (e.g., $8\% = 0.08$)
- **n** = Compounding frequency per year (Daily = 365, Monthly = 12, Quarterly = 4, Annually = 1)
- **t** = Investment tenure in years

#### Step-by-Step Numerical Example:
Suppose you invest **₹10,000** at an annual interest rate of **6.0%** compounded quarterly ($n = 4$) for **5 years**:
1. **P** = 10,000
2. **r** = 0.06
3. **n** = 4
4. **t** = 5 ($n \cdot t = 20$)

Plugging these values into the formula:

$$
A = 10,000 \times \left(1 + \frac{0.06}{4}\right)^{20} = 10,000 \times (1.015)^{20} \approx ₹13,468.55
$$

- **Initial Principal:** ₹10,000
- **Total Interest Earned:** ₹3,468.55

### Simple Interest vs. Compound Interest Comparison

| Feature | Simple Interest | Compound Interest |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Calculation Basis** | Principal only | Principal + Accumulated Interest |
| **Growth Pattern** | Linear growth | Exponential growth |
| **Interest Rate Formula** | $I = P \times r \times t$ | $A = P(1 + r/n)^{nt}$ |
| **Best Suited For** | Short-term loans / bonds | Long-term wealth creation & mutual funds |

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

#### 1. What is the difference between Simple Interest and Compound Interest?
Simple interest is calculated exclusively on the initial principal. Compound interest calculates interest on both the principal and all previously accrued interest, resulting in faster growth.

#### 2. How does compounding frequency affect total returns?
The more frequently interest is compounded (e.g., monthly vs. annually), the higher the total accumulated interest will be over the investment horizon.

#### 3. What is the Rule of 72 in compound interest?
The Rule of 72 is a quick mental math shortcut to estimate how many years it takes to double your money. Divide 72 by the annual interest rate: $\text{Years to Double} \approx 72 / r$.

#### 4. Can compounding work against borrowers on loans?
Yes. On credit cards and compound loans, unpaid interest accumulates onto the principal balance, increasing total debt rapidly if monthly payments are missed.
