### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. How does the Today Date & Time tool determine my local time?
The tool synchronizes your local browser clock with atomic time servers (NTP) to display your exact local time zone offset.

#### 2. What is UTC (Coordinated Universal Time)?
UTC is the primary time standard by which the world regulates clocks and time zones. It does not observe daylight saving time.

#### 3. What is Unix Epoch Time?
Unix Epoch time is the total number of seconds that have elapsed since **January 1, 1970 00:00:00 UTC** (excluding leap seconds).

#### 4. How do time zones work?
Earth is divided into 24 standard geographical time zones spaced roughly 15 degrees of longitude apart, offset from Coordinated Universal Time (UTC+0).

#### 5. What is ISO 8601 date formatting?
ISO 8601 is the international standard date format `YYYY-MM-DD` (e.g., `2026-08-06`), which eliminates ambiguity between US (`MM/DD/YYYY`) and European (`DD/MM/YYYY`) formats.

#### 6. How many days are in a leap year?
A leap year contains **366 days** (adding February 29) to keep calendar years synchronized with the astronomical solar year of ~365.2422 days.

#### 7. What is the International Date Line?
The International Date Line is an imaginary line of longitude located at roughly $180^\circ$ in the Pacific Ocean that marks the boundary between one calendar day and the next.

#### 8. How do I calculate GMT offset?
Subtract your local standard time from GMT/UTC. For example, Indian Standard Time (IST) is $\text{UTC} + 5:30$, and US Eastern Standard Time is $\text{UTC} - 5:00$.
