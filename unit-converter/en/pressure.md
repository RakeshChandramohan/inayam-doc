### Definition
The **Pressure Unit Converter** is an essential engineering tool that provides high-precision conversion across metric, imperial, atmospheric, and barometric pressure units. The SI derived unit for pressure is the **pascal (Pa)**, defined as one newton per square meter ($1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \text{ N/m}^2$).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. What is the SI derived unit for pressure?
The International System of Units (SI) derived unit for pressure is the **pascal (Pa)** ($1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \text{ N/m}^2$).

#### 2. How many pascals (Pa) are in 1 bar?
There are exactly **100,000 pascals** ($100 \text{ kPa}$ or $0.1 \text{ MPa}$) in 1 bar.

#### 3. How many pounds per square inch (PSI) equal 1 bar?
One bar equals approximately **14.5038 PSI**.

#### 4. What is standard atmospheric pressure (1 atm) in PSI and kPa?
Standard atmospheric pressure equals **14.696 PSI** or **101.325 kPa** ($1.01325 \text{ bar}$).

#### 5. What is the recommended PSI for standard automobile tires?
Most passenger vehicle tires require between **30 PSI and 35 PSI** ($2.07 - 2.41 \text{ bar}$).

#### 6. What is the difference between PSIG and PSIA?
- **PSIG (Gauge Pressure):** Pressure relative to ambient atmospheric air ($0 \text{ psig} = 14.7 \text{ psia}$).
- **PSIA (Absolute Pressure):** Total pressure relative to absolute vacuum.

#### 7. How many millimeters of mercury (mmHg / Torr) equal 1 atm?
One standard atmosphere equals exactly **760 mmHg** (760 Torr).

#### 8. How do I convert PSI to Bar mentally?
Divide PSI by 14.5 (or divide by 15 for a rough estimate). For example, $30 \text{ PSI} / 15 = 2.0 \text{ bar}$.
