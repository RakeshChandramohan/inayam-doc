## Financial Health Check Calculator – Evaluate DTI, Savings Rate, and Emergency Buffer

### What is a Financial Health Check?
A **Financial Health Check** is a comprehensive personal finance assessment tool designed to evaluate an individual's financial stability, debt management, emergency preparedness, and long-term retirement readiness.

Using an online **Financial Health Check Calculator** allows individuals to compute critical financial ratios—such as **Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)**, **Savings Rate**, and **Emergency Fund Coverage**—to identify financial vulnerabilities before making major investments.

---

### Core Metrics Evaluated in a Financial Health Check

#### 1. Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)
DTI evaluates the percentage of your gross monthly income dedicated to servicing debt obligations (home loans, car loans, credit cards):

$$
\text{DTI Ratio (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Monthly Debt Payments}}{\text{Gross Monthly Income}} \right) \times 100
$$

- **Healthy DTI Target:** Below **36% - 40%**.

#### 2. Savings Rate Ratio
The Savings Rate measures the percentage of monthly income directed toward savings, mutual funds, and investments:

$$
\text{Savings Rate (\%)} = \left( \frac{\text{Monthly Savings \& Investments}}{\text{Gross Monthly Income}} \right) \times 100
$$

- **Healthy Savings Target:** At least **20%** of gross monthly income.

#### 3. Emergency Fund Coverage (Months)
Measures how many months your liquid cash reserves can cover essential living expenses in the event of job loss or emergency:

$$
\text{Emergency Buffer (Months)} = \frac{\text{Total Liquid Cash Savings}}{\text{Essential Monthly Expenses}}
$$

- **Healthy Emergency Target:** **3 to 6 Months** of living expenses.

### Step-by-Step Numerical Assessment Example

Suppose an individual earns a monthly gross income of **₹80,000**, pays **₹25,000** in total debt EMIs, saves **₹16,000** per month, and has **₹150,000** in emergency liquid savings with essential monthly expenses of **₹40,000**:

1. **Debt-to-Income (DTI):**  
   $$\text{DTI} = \left(\frac{25,000}{80,000}\right) \times 100 = 31.25\% \quad \checkmark \text{ (Healthy)}$$

2. **Savings Rate:**  
   $$\text{Savings Rate} = \left(\frac{16,000}{80,000}\right) \times 100 = 20.0\% \quad \checkmark \text{ (Healthy)}$$

3. **Emergency Fund Coverage:**  
   $$\text{Coverage} = \frac{150,000}{40,000} = 3.75 \text{ Months} \quad \checkmark \text{ (Healthy)}$$

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

#### 1. Why is Debt-to-Income (DTI) ratio critical for loan approvals?
Lenders use DTI to ensure you are not over-leveraged. A DTI above 45% often leads to loan rejection or higher interest rates.

#### 2. What qualifies as liquid emergency fund savings?
Liquid emergency funds include savings account balances, instant-redemption liquid mutual funds, and short-term bank fixed deposits.
