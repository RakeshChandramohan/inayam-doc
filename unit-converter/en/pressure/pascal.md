## Pascal (Pa) Measurement Guide & Pressure Converter

### Definition
The **pascal** (symbol: **Pa**) is the official derived unit of pressure, stress, Young's modulus, and tensile strength in the International System of Units (SI). One pascal is defined as **one newton of force applied perpendicularly over an area of one square meter** ($1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \text{ N/m}^2$).

Because $1 \text{ Pa}$ represents a very small pressure force, scientific and engineering applications frequently utilize SI prefixes such as **kilopascals** ($1 \text{ kPa} = 1,000 \text{ Pa}$), **megapascals** ($1 \text{ MPa} = 1,000,000 \text{ Pa}$), and **gigapascals** ($1 \text{ GPa} = 10^9 \text{ Pa}$).

### How to Calculate Pascal Conversions (Formulas & Examples)

To convert between pascals and other pressure units:

- **Pascals to Bar:** $\text{Bar} = \text{Pa} / 100,000$
- **Pascals to PSI:** $\text{PSI} = \text{Pa} \times 0.0001450377$
- **Pascals to Atmospheres:** $\text{atm} = \text{Pa} / 101,325$
- **Kilopascals to Pascals:** $\text{Pa} = \text{kPa} \times 1,000$

#### Calculation Example:
Standard atmospheric pressure at sea level is **101,325 Pascals**. Convert this value to kilopascals, bar, and PSI:

1. **Kilopascals Conversion:**  
   $$101,325 \text{ Pa} / 1,000 = 101.325 \text{ kPa}$$

2. **Bar Conversion:**  
   $$101,325 \text{ Pa} / 100,000 = 1.01325 \text{ bar}$$

3. **PSI Conversion:**  
   $$101,325 \text{ Pa} \times 0.000145038 \approx 14.70 \text{ PSI}$$

### History and Historical Origin
The pascal was named in 1971 by the General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) to honor **Blaise Pascal**, the 17th-century French mathematician and physicist who conducted groundbreaking experiments on hydrodynamics, barometric pressure, and vacuum principles.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. What is the mathematical definition of 1 Pascal?
One pascal equals one newton of force applied perpendicularly over an area of one square meter ($1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \text{ N/m}^2 = 1 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$).

#### 2. How many pascals are in 1 bar?
There are exactly 100,000 pascals in 1 bar ($1 \text{ bar} = 100 \text{ kPa}$).

#### 3. What is standard atmospheric pressure in Pascals?
Standard atmospheric pressure at sea level ($1 \text{ atm}$) equals exactly **101,325 Pascals** ($101.325 \text{ kPa}$).

#### 4. How many pascals make 1 megapascal (MPa)?
One megapascal ($1 \text{ MPa}$) equals exactly 1,000,000 pascals. Megapascals are widely used in structural engineering to report concrete compression strength and steel tensile strength.

#### 5. How do I convert kilopascals (kPa) to PSI?
Multiply kilopascals by 0.145038. For example, $200 \text{ kPa} \times 0.145038 \approx 29.01 \text{ PSI}$.

#### 6. What is the difference between a Hectopascal (hPa) and a Kilopascal (kPa)?
- $1 \text{ hPa} = 100 \text{ Pa}$ (commonly used in aviation and weather reporting).
- $1 \text{ kPa} = 1,000 \text{ Pa} = 10 \text{ hPa}$.

#### 7. How many PSI are in 1 Pascal?
One pascal equals approximately 0.000145038 PSI ($1 \text{ PSI} \approx 6,894.76 \text{ Pa}$).

#### 8. Why is the Pascal named after Blaise Pascal?
It honors Blaise Pascal's 17th-century experiments proving that atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude using mercury barometers on Puy-de-Dôme mountain.
