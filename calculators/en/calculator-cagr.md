## CAGR Calculator – Calculate Compound Annual Growth Rate

### What is CAGR?
The **Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)** measures the constant annualized rate of return for an investment or business revenue over a multi-year period, assuming compounding growth. Unlike simple arithmetic average returns, CAGR smooths out annual price volatility to provide a single annual growth metric.

Investors use an online **CAGR Calculator** to compare historical returns across stocks, mutual funds, real estate, and business portfolios on an equal footing.

---

### Formula to Calculate CAGR

The mathematical formula to compute CAGR is:

$$
CAGR = \left( \frac{EV}{BV} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1
$$

Where:
- **CAGR** = Compound Annual Growth Rate
- **EV** = Ending Value (Final portfolio or investment balance)
- **BV** = Beginning Value (Initial investment balance)
- **n** = Number of holding years

#### Step-by-Step Numerical Example:
Suppose you invested **₹50,000** in a portfolio 5 years ago, and its total value is now **₹90,000**:
1. **BV** = 50,000
2. **EV** = 90,000
3. **n** = 5 years

Plugging these values into the formula:

$$
CAGR = \left( \frac{90,000}{50,000} \right)^{\frac{1}{5}} - 1 = (1.8)^{0.2} - 1 \approx 0.1247 \text{ or } 12.47\%
$$

This means your investment grew at an average annualized rate of **12.47% per year**.

### Comparison: CAGR vs. Absolute Return vs. XIRR

| Growth Metric | Compounding Factor | Best Used For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CAGR** | Smooths annual growth over $n$ years | Lump-sum investments over fixed periods |
| **Absolute Return** | Simple percentage gain ($[(EV-BV)/BV] \times 100$) | Point-to-point gains under 1 year |
| **XIRR** | Internal rate of return for multiple cash flows | SIPs, mutual fund cash flows, periodic withdrawals |

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

#### 1. What is CAGR and why is it important?
CAGR represents the annualized rate at which an investment grows over time. It is important because it accounts for compounding and allows direct comparisons between different asset classes.

#### 2. How is CAGR different from simple average return?
Simple average return averages yearly percentage changes without considering compounding. CAGR factors in compounding, reflecting the true geometric rate of return.

#### 3. Can CAGR be negative?
Yes. If the ending value of an investment ($EV$) is lower than the beginning value ($BV$), the resulting CAGR will be a negative percentage, indicating annual net loss.

#### 4. What are the limitations of CAGR?
CAGR assumes a smooth, constant growth rate every single year. It ignores market volatility, intra-period drawdowns, and periodic cash inflows or outflows.
