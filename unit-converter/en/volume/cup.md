## Cup Measurement Guide & Culinary Volume Converter

### Definition
The **cup** is a culinary unit of liquid and dry volume used widely in cooking recipes and nutritional portioning.

Under US customary standards, **1 US Cup** equals **8 US fluid ounces**, **16 US tablespoons**, **48 US teaspoons**, or **236.5882365 milliliters**. In metric culinary systems (Australia, New Zealand, UK, Canada, International), **1 Metric Cup** is standardized to exactly **250 milliliters** ($0.25 \text{ L}$).

### How to Calculate Cup Conversions (Formulas & Examples)

To convert between cups and metric or culinary units:

- **US Cups to Milliliters:** $\text{mL} = \text{Cups} \times 236.588237$
- **Metric Cups to Milliliters:** $\text{mL} = \text{Cups} \times 250$
- **US Cups to Fluid Ounces:** $\text{fl oz} = \text{Cups} \times 8$
- **US Cups to Tablespoons:** $\text{tbsp} = \text{Cups} \times 16$

#### Calculation Example:
If a baking recipe calls for **2.5 US cups** of milk, convert the volume to milliliters:

$$\text{Volume (mL)} = 2.5 \text{ cups} \times 236.588 \text{ mL/cup} \approx 591.47 \text{ mL}$$

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. How many milliliters are in 1 US cup?
One US customary cup equals **236.588 milliliters** ($\approx 240 \text{ mL}$).

#### 2. How many milliliters are in 1 Metric cup?
One international metric cup equals exactly **250 milliliters** ($0.25 \text{ L}$).

#### 3. How many fluid ounces (fl oz) are in 1 US cup?
There are exactly **8 US fluid ounces** in 1 US cup.

#### 4. How many tablespoons (tbsp) are in 1 US cup?
There are exactly **16 US tablespoons** in 1 US cup.

#### 5. How many teaspoons (tsp) are in 1 US cup?
There are exactly **48 US teaspoons** ($16 \times 3$) in 1 US cup.

#### 6. How many cups are in 1 US pint?
There are exactly **2 cups** in 1 US pint.

#### 7. How many cups are in 1 US gallon?
There are exactly **16 cups** in 1 US gallon ($4 \text{ quarts} \times 4 \text{ cups/quart}$).

#### 8. What is the difference between measuring dry and liquid ingredients in cups?
Liquid cups measure volume via graduated spout lines, whereas dry cups are designed to be leveled off flat across the top rim.
