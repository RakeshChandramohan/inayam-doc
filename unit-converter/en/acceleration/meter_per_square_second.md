## Meter per Square Second (m/s²) Guide & Acceleration Converter

### Definition
**Meters per square second** (symbol: **m/s²** or $\text{m}\cdot\text{s}^{-2}$) is the official SI derived unit of acceleration, defined as **the rate of change of velocity in meters per second per second**.

One $\text{m/s}^2$ represents an increase in velocity of 1 meter per second every single second. It is the universal standard used in physics, automotive $0-60\text{ mph}$ performance testing, aerospace g-force calculations, structural earthquake shaking analysis, and gravitational field measurements.

### How to Calculate Acceleration Conversions (Formulas & Examples)

To convert between $\text{m/s}^2$, g-force ($g$), and imperial acceleration units:

- **m/s² to Standard Gravity ($g$):** $g = \text{m/s}^2 / 9.80665$
- **Standard Gravity ($g$) to m/s²:** $\text{m/s}^2 = g \times 9.80665$
- **m/s² to Feet per Square Second (ft/s²):** $\text{ft/s}^2 = \text{m/s}^2 \times 3.2808399$
- **Newton's 2nd Law Acceleration Formula:** $a = \frac{F (\text{Newtons})}{m (\text{kg})}$

#### Calculation Example:
If a high-performance supercar accelerates from $0$ to $100 \text{ km/h}$ ($27.78 \text{ m/s}$) in **2.5 seconds**, calculate the average acceleration rate in $\text{m/s}^2$ and g-force ($g$):

1. **Acceleration Calculation ($\text{m/s}^2$):**  
   $$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{27.78 \text{ m/s}}{2.5 \text{ s}} = 11.11 \text{ m/s}^2$$

2. **g-Force Equivalent ($g$):**  
   $$\text{g-Force} = \frac{11.11 \text{ m/s}^2}{9.80665 \text{ m/s}^2/g} \approx 1.13 \text{ } g$$

The International Board of Legal Metrology defines standard acceleration of free fall gravity on Earth's surface as exactly **$9.80665 \text{ m/s}^2$** ($32.17405 \text{ ft/s}^2$).

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

#### 1. What is the definition of 1 m/s²?
One meter per square second represents a change in velocity of 1 meter per second every second.

#### 2. What is Earth standard gravity ($1g$) in m/s²?
Earth standard free-fall gravity ($1g$) equals exactly **9.80665 m/s²** ($\approx 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$).

#### 3. How do I convert m/s² to g-force ($g$)?
Divide the acceleration in m/s² by **9.80665**. For example, $19.62 \text{ m/s}^2 / 9.80665 = 2g$.

#### 4. How many feet per square second (ft/s²) are in 1 m/s²?
One m/s² equals approximately **3.28084 feet per square second** ($1g = 32.174 \text{ ft/s}^2$).

#### 5. What is Moon surface gravity in m/s²?
Moon surface gravity is approximately **1.62 m/s²** ($\approx 0.166g$ or $\frac{1}{6}$ of Earth's gravity).

#### 6. What is Mars surface gravity in m/s²?
Mars surface gravity is approximately **3.71 m/s²** ($\approx 0.378g$ or $38\%$ of Earth's gravity).

#### 7. How do I calculate acceleration from force ($F = m \cdot a$)?
Divide force in newtons by mass in kilograms: $a = \frac{F}{m}$.

#### 8. How do I convert m/s² to km/h per second mentally?
Multiply by 3.6. For example, $5 \text{ m/s}^2 \times 3.6 = 18 \text{ km/h per second}$.
